Smart Healthcare Analytics: Patient Risk & Lifestyle Insights

Tableau-style guided narrative presentation exploring demographic distribution, chronic conditions, and behavioral intervention.

5 Story Scenes Tableau Desktop Guide
Scene 1 of 5 · Demographic Baseline

Understanding Cohort Demographics

The foundation of our analysis rests on 5,000 patient records distributed across six distinct life stages. Teenagers and young adults comprise 36.3% of the surveyed population, providing critical insight into early prevention opportunities.

Analytical Focus: Establishes unbiased age representation before examining condition prevalence in subsequent scenes.
Scene 2 of 5 · Symptomatic Burden

Fatigue as a Universal Clinical Indicator

Contrary to common assumptions that fatigue is primarily an elderly complaint, reported fatigue remains remarkably steady (~400 to 440 cases) across all age tiers from teenagers to seniors.

Clinical Note: Cross-tabulation links persistent fatigue strongly to sedentary behavior and early metabolic dysregulation.
Scene 3 of 5 · Modifiable Lifestyle

Physical Inactivity vs Active Habits

Physical activity levels are evenly segmented between Active (33.52%), Moderate (33.54%), and Sedentary (32.94%). One out of three patients lives an entirely sedentary lifestyle.

Public Health Implication: Sedentary lifestyle constitutes the single most accessible target for preventive clinical intervention.
Scene 4 of 5 · Vascular Assessment

The Hypertension Paradox Across Ages

While the Old cohort records the highest average BP (135.67 mmHg), Young Adults (134.67 mmHg) and Teenagers (134.50 mmHg) sit shockingly close, all averaging in the Stage 1 Hypertension range.

Takeaway: High blood pressure is not merely an affliction of the elderly, but a silent epidemic emerging in youth.
Scene 5 of 5 · Synthesis & Recommendation

Compounding Risk & Exercise Shield

This matrix demonstrates the compounding interaction of body mass and physical exertion. While Obese + Sedentary individuals experience a peak 42.86% heart disease rate, active exercise consistently reduces cardiovascular morbidity by ~5% across all weight brackets.

Evidence-Based Policy: Encouraging moderate to active physical exercise acts as an immediate risk mitigation strategy even before significant weight loss is attained.
